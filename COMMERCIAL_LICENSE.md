# Commercial Use Policy

ShipFlow is open-source software released under the [MIT License](./LICENSE).

## What is free — always

- Self-hosting ShipFlow for your own team or organization
- Internal commercial use (your company uses ShipFlow to manage your own projects)
- Building integrations, plugins, or tools that connect to ShipFlow's API
- Forking and modifying ShipFlow for your own use
- Using ShipFlow in consulting engagements for a client's internal deployment

## What requires a commercial license

**Offering ShipFlow as a hosted service to third parties** — meaning your customers
log into a ShipFlow instance that you operate on their behalf — requires a commercial license.

Examples that require a commercial license:
- A SaaS product where your customers pay to use a hosted ShipFlow
- A managed ShipFlow offering bundled into another product
- A white-labeled version of ShipFlow sold to end users

## How to get a commercial license

Contact: **farzad@shipflow.dev**

We offer flexible terms for startups, agencies, and enterprises. Revenue-based
pricing is available so early-stage companies can get started without large upfront costs.

## Why this policy

ShipFlow is built and maintained by a solo developer. The MIT license lets everyone
self-host freely — that will never change. This policy simply ensures that if someone
builds a business by reselling ShipFlow as a service, the project that makes it possible
is sustainable too.
