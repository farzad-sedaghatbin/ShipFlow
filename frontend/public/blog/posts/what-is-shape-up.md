## Overview

Shape Up is a product development methodology created by Basecamp (the company behind Basecamp and Hey). It was originally shared as a free book at [basecamp.com/shapeup](https://basecamp.com/shapeup) and has since been adopted by many product teams.

Unlike Scrum or Kanban, Shape Up has no daily standups, no sprint backlog, and no burn-down charts. Instead it operates on **six-week cycles** with defined pitches, a betting table, and a powerful visual progress tracker called the **hill chart**.

## The core ideas

### Shaping work before betting on it

Before a feature enters a cycle, it goes through a **shaping** phase where senior staff (designers, engineers, product leads) define the *problem* and the *appetite* — how much time is worth spending. Shaped work includes rough scope and key design decisions, but deliberately leaves the "how" to the team.

### Betting, not backlogs

At the end of each six-week cycle, stakeholders hold a **betting table** meeting. They bet on which shaped pitches will be built in the next cycle. There is no grooming, no estimation poker, no backlog to maintain. Unpicked pitches simply expire — they don't pile up.

### Hill charts

Progress is tracked with hill charts. Each scope (a chunk of work within a pitch) is a dot on a hill: the uphill side represents figuring out the approach, the downhill side represents execution. This gives everyone a feel for *confidence*, not just completion percentages.

### Cooldown

After each six-week cycle there's a **two-week cooldown** period. No new feature work. Teams use it to fix bugs, write documentation, explore ideas, and recharge.
